Alright, listen up, you fun-coupon throwers.

**The Wolf of Wall Street** is the ultimate three-hour spectacle of pure, unadulterated, grade-A chaos. It tells the true story of Jordan Belfort, a hungry kid from Queens who arrives on Wall Street with a dream and works out almost immediately that the game was never about picking winning stocks. It's about moving money from the client's pocket into yours.

Black Monday wipes out his legitimate brokerage job. He lands at a penny-stock joint in a Long Island strip mall, realises these buyers will take anything if you talk fast enough and sound like a god, and Stratton Oakmont is born. Alongside him is Donnie Azoff — a man who married his cousin and is not remotely ashamed to say so — and together they build an empire running on cocaine, Quaaludes and raw capitalist greed.

It is not a film about high finance. It is a film about what happens when you hand a twenty-six-year-old forty-nine million dollars in a single year and his brain turns into a circus.

## The vibe: absolute debauchery

This movie does not crawl. It sprints.

DiCaprio gives a performance so unhinged he essentially invents a new genre of physical comedy in the Quaalude sequence. Watching him drag a paralysed body down a flight of stairs toward a Ferrari is peak cinema. You genuinely do not know whether to applaud or call an ambulance, and the film has no interest in helping you decide.

## The dynamic duo

The chemistry between DiCaprio and Jonah Hill is comedic gold. Hill in those fake white teeth — throwing punches, eating a live goldfish, casually valuing his own watch mid-argument — is iconic.

They are not traditional protagonists. They are glorious, degenerate scumbags, and you cannot look away from either of them.

## McConaughey's ten-minute masterclass

McConaughey is on screen for maybe ten minutes as Mark Hanna and owns the entire philosophy of the film. The chest-thumping, the humming, the restaurant lunch that functions as the movie's mission statement.

> "It's a fugazi. It doesn't exist."

That's the thesis. Everything after it is elaboration.

## The downfall

When Agent Denham comes knocking on the yacht, the dialogue turns sharper than anything else in the film. Jordan tries to buy him off with lobster and caviar and increasingly unsubtle bribes, and Denham simply does not move.

Watching the empire collapse under the weight of its own absurdity is satisfying — right up to the last scene, where Scorsese makes sure you remember the actual ending. Men like Jordan land on their feet. He's selling seminars now, to a room full of people who want to be him.

## Final verdict

The Wolf of Wall Street is a dark, hilarious mirror held up to American greed. It never lectures you and never hands you the moral. It lets the madness rip and trusts you to do your own judging.

**9.6 / 10.** Start thumping your chest and pump those numbers up.
