The image gives you a garden. Flowers, a greenhouse, children playing, a wife pleased with her borders.

The track gives you the camp.

## The load-bearing element

Most films use sound to reinforce what the picture already says. Here it carries information the picture actively withholds, and the audience assembles a third thing that neither element depicts.

That is not a flourish. It is the only way to make this film without staging the atrocity, and staging it was never an option that a serious filmmaker had available.

## Why the cameras were locked off

Glazer shot much of the domestic material on fixed rigs running simultaneously, with the crew out of the rooms. The result reads as surveillance rather than coverage — nobody is being framed for sympathy, because nobody is being framed at all.

It also means the performances have nowhere to hide. There is no reaction shot to cut to, so the banality has to play in full.

## Mica Levi's two minutes

The score barely exists, which makes the moments it does appear function as punctuation rather than mood. Levi did the same thing on Under the Skin from the opposite direction: there, the strings were the most present element in the film. Here they are the rarest.

Both choices come from the same instinct — decide what the sound is *for*, then refuse to use it for anything else.

## The test

Mute it. There is no version of this film that survives that, and there are very few films about which this is genuinely true.
